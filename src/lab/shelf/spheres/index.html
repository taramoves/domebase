“The sphere is the interior, disclosed, shared realm inhabited by humans in so far as they succeed in becoming humans.”

Peter Sloterdijk, Spheres I: Bubbles — Microspherology 28

the scene the book opens with

1 the child and the soap bubbles

  • “There is a solidarity between the soap bubble and its blower that excludes the rest of the world.” 18
  • “In the ecstasy of attentiveness, the child's consciousness has virtually left its corporeal source.” 18
  • “the experimenting player plunges into the open space and transforms the zone between the eye and the object into an animated sphere” 19
  • “that the spirit, in its own way, is in space” 19
  • “Who first had the thought that the world is nothing but the soap bubble of an all-encompassing breath?” 20

what a sphere is

2 the interior, shared

  • “Because living always means building spheres, both on a small and a large scale, humans are the beings that establish globes and look out into horizons.” 28
  • “What is here termed a sphere is, in a first and provisional understanding, an orb in two halves, polarized and differentiated from the start, yet nonetheless intimately joined, subjective and subject to experience — a biune shared space of present and past experience.” 45
  • “spheres are by definition also morpho-immunological constructs” 46
  • “They flourish only in the greenhouse of their autogenous atmosphere.” 46
  • “Humans have never lived in a direct relationship with “nature,” and their cultures have certainly never set foot in the realm of what we call the bare facts” 46

3 why the trilogy is called this

  • “The first book of this sphere trilogy speaks of microspheric units that will be referred to here as bubbles. They constitute the intimate forms of the rounded being-in-form and the basic molecule of the strong relationship.” 62
  • “love stories are stories of form, and that every act of solidarity is an act of sphere formation, that is to say the creation of an interior” 12

the modern condition

4 a core without a shell

  • “What makes the Modern Age special is that after the turn to the Copernican world, the sky as an immune system was suddenly useless.” 25
  • “Modernity is characterized by the technical production of its immunities and the increasing removal of its safety structures from the traditional theological and cosmological narratives.” 25
  • “modern people have had to learn how one goes about existing as a core without a shell” 23
  • “Taking part in modernity means putting evolved immune systems at risk.” 23

the method

5 microspherology, or thinking the interior

  • “Humans are beings that participate in spaces unknown to physics” 83
  • “the human space as an intertwining of several interior spaces; here the surreal becomes the real” 87
  • “Intimacy is the realm of surreal autogenous containers.” 88
  • “intimacy, beyond its first sugary experience, can only be understood as an inscrutability within the most obvious” 90
  • “It is the basic neurosis of Western culture to have to dream of a subject that watches, names and owns everything, without letting anything contain, appoint or own it” 86
