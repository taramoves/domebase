The dome as a delivery format: the master, the projectors, the loudspeakers.

1 The Master

A dome master holds a hemisphere in a square frame, 720, 1024, 1536, 2048, 3200, 3600 or 4096 px a side. The picture fills the circle inscribed in the square and black fills the corners, carrying the show name, the timecode and the copyright. Frames travel as numbered PNG or TARGA with RLE, or as JPEG at the highest quality, 8, 10 or 12 bits a colour, at 30 or 60 fps; 24, 25, 29.97, 48 and 59.94 turn up too.

The projection is equidistant azimuthal: distance from the centre of the circle is the angle from the zenith, so the rings of latitude sit evenly spaced. In a 4096 master the rim is 2048 px from the centre and 45° sits at 1024 px. The frame reaches 180° from the zenith to the horizon and covers all 360° of azimuth. The front of the dome lands at the bottom of the frame, the back at the top, and the right of the frame answers to the right of a viewer at the centre of the room. Action reads from where it sits: IMERSA puts the safe area within ±50° of longitude around the front and 10–60° of altitude, on a camera tilted 15°.

Source: IMERSA and AFDI, Dome Master Specifications, 2019 — imersa.org/guidelines.

2 Picture, Master to Dome

A player reads the master and puts it on the dome. A file plays the numbered frames or the movie; a live render sends each frame out as NDI over the network, or as SDI and HDMI on a cable.

No projector covers a hemisphere on its own, so the show machine splits the master into one patch per projector, warps each patch onto the part of the dome its projector sees, and blends the band where two patches overlap. The room decides the split and the warp: where the projectors stand, the radius of the dome and the throw of each lens go into a mesh the GPU applies to every frame.

3 Sound, File to Loudspeakers

A spatial mix carries one channel per loudspeaker: eight, sixteen, or the room's own count. The room decodes those channels onto its speaker positions, or the sources carry positions and the software decodes them live.

SpatGRIS takes positions over OSC from ControlGRIS, places each source on the speaker setup drawn in the software, and writes one gain per speaker: a source becomes gains across the speakers nearest it, so the sound travels as the position moves. DOME pans that way for speaker domes; CUBE pans by matrix base amplitude for rings, halls and theatres; HYBRID sits between the two; BINAURAL and STEREO fold the mix down to headphones or two channels.

The output leaves through the audio interface, so its channel count sets how many loudspeakers the room can drive: one output per speaker, through amplifiers, into the rings at the dome's elevations. A festival takes the mix as stereo, 5.1 or 7.1.

Source: GRIS, Université de Montréal and SAT — github.com/GRIS-UdeM/SpatGRIS.

4 Glossary

BINAURAL. A head-related transfer function mixdown, for headphones.

channel order. Which speaker each of the file's channels and each of the interface's outputs answers to.

ControlGRIS. A plugin that sends source positions and trajectories to SpatGRIS over OSC, driven from a DAW.

CUBE. SpatGRIS's mode for rings, halls and theatres: matrix base amplitude panning.

DOME. SpatGRIS's mode for speaker domes. Vector base amplitude panning gives a source gains across the speakers nearest it.

dome master. A square file holding the hemisphere: the picture fills the circle inscribed in it, black fills the rest.

edge blend. Two projectors' patches overlap at their seam; the blend fades one into the other.

equidistant azimuthal. The master's projection. Distance from the centre is the angle from the zenith, so the rings of latitude sit evenly spaced.

HYBRID. DOME's panning with CUBE's behaviour, for setups between the two.

interface. The box that turns the software's channels into analogue, MADI or Dante outputs, one per speaker.

NDI. Picture over the network as a stream, rather than a file on disk.

OSC. Open Sound Control: positions, trajectories and cues sent as messages over the network.

SDI. A broadcast coax cable carrying an uncompressed picture between machines.

SpatGRIS. Spatialisation software from GRIS and SAT. It reads source positions over OSC, decodes them onto the speaker setup you draw in it — circles, grids, polyhedrons — and writes one output channel per speaker, 256 in and 256 out.

speaker ring. The speakers at one elevation around the dome; a room adds further rings above it.

STEREO. A two-channel mixdown of the spatial mix.

warp. The mesh that bends each projector's patch onto the part of the dome its projector sees.
